# For the old crew

Hey — I made a thing and I want you six to be the first to kick the tires.

It's called **Speak, Memory**. It's a journal, but not the kind you fall behind on and feel guilty about. You just **talk** — dictate a few sentences about your day, or dredge up something from 1974 — and it quietly does the boring part: it writes the summaries.

Here's the part I think you'll actually like. Everything you put in gets **zoomable**. A day, a whole decade, a person — each one distills down to a single word, then a phrase, then a sentence, and if you want the whole thing you tap once and it opens up. So you can skim your life at arm's length, or dive into one afternoon. Your own words are always there underneath, untouched.

You can file the old stuff by **category and subject** — "girlfriends → Deena," "schools → Roosevelt High," "the Elm St. house" — and it lays them out on a **timeline** across the years. There's even a little map of the whole thing (a "Graph") where you can watch it think.

It runs in your phone's browser and everything stays **on your device** — this isn't going in a feed anywhere. No accounts, no ads, nobody's mining it. It's just for you (and whatever you decide to read aloud at the next reunion).

If a summary gets something wrong — a name, a year, two stories smushed together — you tell it *why* and it fixes it, and remembers the correction.

Try this: open it, hit **Write**, and just say what you did today. Then open **"A past memory?"** and put in one good story from way back. Give it a minute and go look in the **Journal**. That's the whole pitch.

Tell me what breaks, what's confusing, and what you wish it did. You're the beta test.

— J.
