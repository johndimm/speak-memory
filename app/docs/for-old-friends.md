# For the old crew

Hey, I made a thing and I want you six to be the first to kick the tires.

It's called **Speak, Memory**. It's a journal, but not the kind you fall behind on and feel guilty about. You just **talk**: say a few sentences about your day and it quietly does the boring part, writing the summaries.

The part I think you'll like: everything you put in becomes **zoomable**. A day, a year, a whole decade, your whole life: each one distills down to a single word, then a phrase, then a sentence, and you tap once for the whole story. So you can skim your life at arm's length or dive into one afternoon. Your own words are always one tap away underneath.

Then it gets personal:

- **Me:** you just talk, and it asks one question at a time (where do you live, since when, who with, what you do for fun) and fills in the answers as you go.
- **Names:** everyone you mention becomes a link. It writes a short description of the people who come up a lot and asks you about the rest.
- **Stories:** the old stuff, laid out as timelines: Homes, Relationships, Jobs, Schools. Tap the empty box at the left end and it asks, "Where did you live before Elm St.? When?"
- **Futures:** this is the fun one. It imagines your journal carrying on from tomorrow, for ten years, twenty, or all the way to 95, in your own voice, with your real people. You can steer it ("what if I move to the coast") and even hear it read aloud.

It runs in your phone's browser and everything stays **on your device**. No accounts, no ads, nobody's mining it. It's just for you (and whatever you decide to read aloud at the next reunion).

Try this: open it, tap **Journal**, and just say what you did today. Then open **Browse** and walk up to **Life** to see what single word it dares to use. That's the whole pitch.

Tell me what breaks, what's confusing, and what you wish it did. You're the beta test.

— J.
