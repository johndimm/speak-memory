# A demo: watching a life take shape

Meet **Nora Fenn**, born 1951. She just started keeping *Speak, Memory*. Follow her through the four levels of use, and keep one eye on the very top of the tree, the **Life** page in **Browse**. There, a single **word**, a **phrase** and a **sentence** try to hold everything she's put in so far. Watch them change.

*(The summaries below are the kind the app writes. What matters is how they move as more of a life goes in.)*

---

## Level 1: Just the Journal

### Day one: an ordinary Tuesday

She opens **Journal**, taps **🎤 Dictate**, and talks for a minute: coffee on the porch, deadheading the roses, a call from her grandson Sam about his science fair. **Save.** Within seconds the day has its own ladder, and so does everything above it: the week, the month, the year, the decade, and Life.

> **Life**
> **word:** Tuesday
> **phrase:** A quiet morning at home
> **sentence:** An unremarkable, contented day of coffee, the garden, and a grandson's phone call.

A life summarized from one day looks like one day. Fair enough.

She keeps it up for a couple of weeks: short entries, some rambling, one where she just complains about the heat. She never reads her own transcripts back. She reads the summaries, which are cleaner than what she said.

> **Life**
> **word:** Tending
> **phrase:** Garden, shop, grandson
> **sentence:** A woman in her seventies keeping a garden, a small bookshop, and a close eye on her grandson Sam.

Already the app has noticed names: **Sam**, **Fennwick Books**, **Marisol** (who helps at the shop). They light up under **Found** as she talks, and each one becomes a link.

---

## Level 2: Me and Names

### Me

The **Next** button nudges her to **Me**. She just talks, and one question at a time appears above the box: *Where do you live, and since when?* She answers: "Same house on Alder Street since 1976, alone since Daniel died, I run the bookshop, I was born in '51." The chips tick themselves off: **Born 1951 · Home: Alder Street, since 1976 · Work: Fennwick Books, since 1978**.

### Names

Then **Names**. Sam and Marisol come up often, so the app writes their descriptions from her entries. A few are dashed, mentioned only once: **Daniel**, **the Platte**. **Next name to describe →** walks her through them. For Daniel she says: "My husband. Forty years. He came in for a used atlas in 1974 and stayed." That one sentence is now how the app knows him, everywhere.

---

## Level 3: Stories (patching in the past)

**Stories** opens with her life, thread by thread. Each thread is a row of blocks ending in **Now**, which comes straight from Me:

> **Homes:** [ ＋ ] [ **Now** · Alder Street · since 1976 ]
> **Jobs:** [ ＋ ] [ **Now** · Fennwick Books · since 1978 ]

She taps **＋** on Homes and the box asks: *Where did you live before Alder Street? When did you move there?* She tells it about **Chicago, 1970**: nineteen, a bus ticket, waitressing doubles, the folk clubs on the North Side. Then ＋ again, before Chicago: **the Nebraska farm**, her father who spoke mostly to the animals, the brown river she wasn't allowed near and swam in anyway.

> **Life**
> **word:** Roots
> **phrase:** From a silent farm to a shop of books
> **sentence:** A girl who left a quiet Nebraska farm for Chicago's folk clubs, and settled into a long life of books and tending.

The word jumped from "Tending" to "Roots." Same person; the app can just *see* further now.

She fills in more: **Relationships → Daniel, 1974–2010**; the lean years at the shop, the flood of '93 that took the bottom two shelves and a wedding album; the last winter, and opening the shop alone the next spring because she didn't know what else to do with her hands.

> **Life**
> **word:** Kept
> **phrase:** Still keeping things alive
> **sentence:** A farm girl who ran toward music and spent a life keeping things alive (a marriage, a shop, a family, a few borrowed songs), and is quietly still at it.

She never said "keeping things alive." The app found that thread across a farm, a marriage, a flood, a shop and a loss.

---

## Level 4: A Future

Now all of that pays off. In **Futures** she taps **＋ New future**, leaves the prompt blank, picks **To 95**, and taps **Imagine ›**. A minute later the new Future shows **Open ›**.

It opens in **Browse** at Life. A banner reads **🔮 Imagined future · Sep 28, 2026 → 2046 · "Straight ahead"**. Her real decades run straight into the imagined ones: the 1950s farm, the 1970s Chicago clubs, the long middle with Daniel, and then 2030s entries in her own voice. Sam graduates, Marisol eventually takes over the shop, and the roses are still being deadheaded, more slowly. The imagined years keep everyone exactly as she described them: Daniel is still her husband, and Sam is still her grandson.

> **Life (in the Future)**
> **word:** Handed-on
> **phrase:** The keeping passes on
> **sentence:** A woman who spent her life keeping things alive, and in her last years quietly taught others to keep them.

She tries another with a prompt, *"what if I sell the shop next year"*, and compares the two. Both sit in one list on the **Futures** page, with **Now · Your journal** at the top to switch back.

---

## What just happened

- **The word** bounced around: *Tuesday, Tending, Roots, Kept, Handed-on.* Each is an honest stab at "in one word…", and none of them is a life. That's the joke, and it's a good one to check in on.
- **The sentence** did something else. As more of Nora went in, it stopped listing and started *finding the thread*.
- **Each level fed the next.** The Journal gave it her present, Me and Names told it who's who, and Stories gave it her past. The Future could only sound like her because of all three.

*Try it yourself: say one honest thing about today in **Journal**, then open **Browse**, walk up to **Life**, and see what single word it dares to use.*
